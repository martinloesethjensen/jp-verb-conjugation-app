# Counters: design

Status: drafted in chat 2026-10-17, pending the owner's review.

## Problem

Learners know they need a counter (本 for pencils, 枚 for paper). What they get wrong is
the reading: さんぼん, not さんほん; いっぽん, not いちほん; ひとり, not いちにん. The sound
changes cluster on a few numbers (1, 3, 6, 8, 10 and 何) and follow a handful of patterns
by the counter's first sound, with a few fully irregular counters on top.

So the unit worth teaching and tracking is a **counter with a number** (3本 → さんぼん), not
the counter alone. The app has no counters today.

## Decisions

- **A fourth tab,** Counters, between Grammar and Word Bank. Counters are a reading
  system with their own lookup, not a grammar pattern.
- **18 N5 counters in milestone 1:** つ, 人, 本, 杯, 匹, 分, 個, 回, 階, 冊, 歳, 枚,
  台, 円, 時, 年, and 日 twice (day of the month, number of days). Together they cover
  every sound class and the main irregulars, and are few enough to review by hand.
- **Groups.** The tab opens on a grid of counting groups (Drinks, People, Days & dates,
  Years & age, …). Each group opens a lookup table comparing its counters side by side.
  A counter can be in several groups (本 is in Drinks and Long things).
- **One search for everything:** groups, counters, things, and counted forms. 3本, 三本,
  さんぼん and "sanbon" all find 本 at 3.
- **Readings are generated in the data pipeline,** like adjective forms: authors write a
  sound class and any exceptions, and `update_data.py` fills every reading. The app has no
  reading logic.
- **Numbers 1–10 and 何 only.** All the irregularity is there. Compound numbers (21本) are
  out of scope, so the app never composes readings.
- **The playground ships in milestone 1,** as a card on each counter page.
- **Quiz attempts stay in one table.** `QuizAttempt.verb` is renamed `subject` and holds a
  counter id for counter questions.
- **Three milestones,** each shipping on its own: 1 reference and playground, 2 counter
  quiz, 3 sound patterns and widget.

## Design

### Sound classes

Base numbers: いち に さん よん ご ろく なな はち きゅう じゅう, and なん for 何. The
classes say which of them change:

| Class | Counters (m1) | 1 | 3 | 4 | 6 | 8 | 10 | 何 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `plain` | 枚, 台, 円, 年 | いち | さん | よん | ろく | はち | じゅう | なん |
| `k` | 個, 回, 階 | いっ | さん | よん | ろっ | はっ, はち | じゅっ, じっ | なん |
| `s` | 冊, 歳 | いっ | さん | よん | ろく | はっ | じゅっ, じっ | なん |
| `h-b` | 本, 杯, 匹 | いっ+p | さん+b | よん | ろっ+p | はっ+p, はち | じゅっ+p, じっ+p | なん+b |
| `h-p` | 分 | いっ+p | さん+p | よん+p | ろっ+p | はっ+p, はち | じゅっ+p, じっ+p | なん+p |
| `irregular` | つ, 人, 時, 日 ×2 | written in full by the author | | | | | | |

- `+p` and `+b` turn the counter's は-row sound into ぱ or ば (ほん → ぽん, ぼん).
- 2, 5, 7 and 9 never change in these classes, and 7 is always なな.
- The first reading in a list is the preferred one. はちほん and じっぽん are accepted
  but never shown first.
- Exceptions inside a class are overrides: 円 4 is よえん; 年 4 is よねん and 7 is
  ななねん or しちねん; 階 3 and 何 are さんがい and なんがい, with さんかい and なんかい
  accepted.
- `irregular` counters have no generated readings. Their `overrides` must cover all 11
  keys:
  - つ: ひとつ … とお, 何 いくつ
  - 人: ひとり, ふたり, よにん, with しちにん as a second reading for 7
  - 時: よじ, しちじ, くじ
  - 日 (day of the month, `nichi-date`): ついたち, ふつか, みっか, よっか, いつか,
    むいか, なのか, ようか, ここのか, とおか, 何 なんにち
  - 日 (number of days, `nichi-days`): いちにち, then the same as the date counter
    from ふつか to とおか, 何 なんにち
- The two 日 counters share their written form, so 一日 means ついたち in one and いちにち
  in the other. The difference is a pitfall on both pages, and examples say which reading
  they use (see `readings` on examples below).

### Data

**`data/counters.json`** (new; hand-authored except `readings` and `changed`):

```json
{
  "version": "1.0.0",
  "description": "Japanese counters. readings and changed are generated by scripts/update_data.py.",
  "counters": [
    { "id": "hon", "counter": "本", "reading": "ほん", "meaning": "long, thin things",
      "jlpt": "N5", "sound": "h-b", "symbol": "pencil",
      "overrides": {},
      "readings": { "1": ["いっぽん"], "2": ["にほん"], "3": ["さんぼん"], "4": ["よんほん"],
                    "5": ["ごほん"], "6": ["ろっぽん"], "7": ["ななほん"],
                    "8": ["はっぽん", "はちほん"], "9": ["きゅうほん"],
                    "10": ["じゅっぽん", "じっぽん"], "what": ["なんぼん"] },
      "changed": ["1", "3", "6", "8", "10", "what"],
      "things": [ { "word": "鉛筆", "kana": "えんぴつ", "meaning": "pencil", "fit": "primary", "symbol": "pencil" },
                  { "word": "傘", "kana": "かさ", "meaning": "umbrella", "fit": "primary", "symbol": "umbrella" },
                  { "word": "ビール", "kana": "ビール", "meaning": "beer (bottles)", "fit": "primary", "symbol": "waterbottle" } ],
      "examples": [ { "jp": "ビールを二本ください。", "en": "Two bottles of beer, please." } ],
      "pitfalls": [ { "heading": "Not さんほん", "explanation": "…", "examples": [] } ],
      "related": ["hai", "hiki"] }
  ]
}
```

- `sound` is a class from the table above. `overrides` maps a key (`"1"`…`"10"`,
  `"what"`) to readings that replace the generated ones.
- `readings` is generated: the class's rule, then overrides. `changed` is generated too:
  the keys whose preferred reading is not the base number plus the counter's own reading.
  It is what the page and playground highlight, so the app does not decide what changed.
  `irregular` counters get an empty `changed`: every cell would differ, and a tint on
  every cell says nothing. Their pages say "Irregular: learn each reading" instead.
- `things` are plain nouns with no forms. `fit` is `primary`, `acceptable` (rabbits under
  匹 when 羽 is primary) or `casual` (the つ or 個 fallback). A thing may appear under
  several counters. `symbol` (an SF Symbol name) is optional and falls back to the
  counter's `symbol`.
- `examples` take the grammar example shape plus an optional `readings` map
  (`{"一日": "ついたち"}`). Its entries take priority over `furigana.json` for that
  example, because one dictionary key can't read 一日 two ways. `pitfalls` take the
  grammar pitfall shape, but their examples take `readings` too (`CounterPitfall`).
- **`groups`** is a top-level array next to `counters`, in grid order:

  ```json
  "groups": [
    { "id": "drinks", "title": "Drinks", "symbol": "cup.and.saucer",
      "summary": "Glasses and cups, bottles, or just \"one, two\" when ordering.",
      "members": [ { "counter": "hai", "note": "glasses, cups, bowls" },
                   { "counter": "hon", "note": "bottles and cans" },
                   { "counter": "tsu", "note": "ordering casually" } ] }
  ]
  ```

  Groups in milestone 1:

  | Group | Counters |
  |---|---|
  | People | 人 |
  | Animals | 匹 |
  | Drinks | 杯, 本, つ |
  | Long things | 本 |
  | Flat things | 枚 |
  | Small things | 個, つ |
  | Books | 冊 |
  | Machines & vehicles | 台 |
  | Clock time | 時, 分 |
  | Days & dates | 日 (date), 日 (days) |
  | Years & age | 年, 歳 |
  | Money | 円 |
  | Times & floors | 回, 階 |

  A member's `note` says what the counter covers within that group.
- `update_data.py` checks:
  - every counter has all 11 readings and every reading is hiragana
  - `sound` is known, and `irregular` counters override every key
  - override keys are valid
  - `related` ids resolve, and `fit` and `jlpt` values are known
  - group ids are unique, members resolve, and every counter is in at least one group
  - every counted run in an example (三本, 一日) reads as one of that counter's readings
    for that number, through the example's own `readings` first, then `furigana.json`
  - `--check` fails when `readings` or `changed` are stale
- The furigana coverage check includes `counters.json`. Example sentences need their
  counted runs in `furigana.json` (二本 → にほん), because the dictionary on its own would
  read 本 as ほん.
- New manifest entry `counters` (version and sha256), signed like the others. A
  `CountersSyncService` and storage follow the `GrammarSyncService` pattern.

### VerbKit

- `Counter`, `CountedThing`, `CounterDataFile` and `CounterFit` models. Old app builds
  ignore the new manifest key.
- `CounterNumber`: `.n(1...10)` or `.what`, with the JSON key and the display form
  (三本, 何本).
- `Counter.readings(for:) -> [String]` and `Counter.isChanged(_:)` only read data.
- `CounterGroup` and `CounterGroupMember` models, decoded from `groups`.
- `CounterSearch` returns results in four sections:
  - **Groups:** title and summary ("drinks", "time").
  - **Counters:** the counter, its reading and its meaning.
  - **Forms:** counted forms. Arabic digits become kanji numerals (3本 → 三本, 10本 → 十本)
    and romaji becomes hiragana (sanbon → さんぼん). A form matches when its written form
    equals the query or its reading starts with it (at least 2 kana). Every accepted
    reading counts, so はちほん finds 本 at 8.
  - **Things:** word, kana and meaning, each returning its counters, primary first.
- `Route.counter(String)` with `verbtable://counter/<id>`, and `Route.counterGroup(String)`
  with `verbtable://counters/<id>`. `RouteTarget` gains both. Inside the tab, opening a
  counter can carry a focus number, so a Forms result lands on the page with the
  playground set to 3 and that cell highlighted.
- Ruby for readings cells comes from `counters.json`, never from `FuriganaDictionary`.
- Spotlight indexes counters and their things ("umbrella" → 本).
- Milestone 2:
  - `QuizAttempt.verb` → `subject`, with `@Attribute(originalName: "verb")` on the entity
    (lightweight migration).
  - New `QuizQuestionKind` cases: `counterChoice` (formID = the thing's word) and
    `counterReading` (formID = the number key).
  - A counter question builder and Weak spots for `counterReading` pairs.

### App

**Counters tab** (`textformat.123`), a `NavigationSplitView` like Grammar:

- **Home: the groups grid.** An adaptive grid of tiles (two columns on iPhone), each
  showing the group's symbol, its title and its counters (杯 · 本 · つ). The last tile is
  "All counters", which opens the full list grouped by level. Counters hidden by the JLPT
  settings are left out of tiles and tables, and a group with none left is hidden.
- **Group page: the lookup table.** Numbers run down the rows (1–10, 何) and the group's
  counters run across the columns, so groups of up to three fit an iPhone without
  scrolling; wider groups scroll sideways with the number column pinned. Column headers
  show the counter, its reading and the member `note`. Cells show the preferred reading
  with the written form small above it. Changed cells get the same tint and dot as the
  counter page. Tapping a cell speaks it, long-pressing copies it, and tapping a header
  opens that counter. The group `summary` sits above the table.
- **Search** is on the home screen and uses `CounterSearch`. With a query, the grid is
  replaced by the four sections (Groups, Counters, Forms, Things). A Forms row reads
  "三本 さんぼん · 本 at 3"; a Things row reads "傘 · umbrella → 本".
- **Counter page,** in this order:
  1. Header: 本, ほん, "long, thin things", symbol, speaker.
  2. **Count them** (playground): a 1–10 stepper (and a 何 chip), a row of the thing's
     symbol repeated n times, and the reading below it, large, with 三本 small above.
     When the number is in `changed`, the whole reading is tinted (the data does not say
     which kana changed) and the cell gets a dot marker, so the change does not rely on colour alone. Changes animate (いっぽん →
     にほん → さんぼん). A thing picker swaps the symbol. Tapping the reading speaks it.
  3. **Readings:** a grid of 1–10 and 何 with `changed` cells marked. Tap to speak,
     long-press to copy (the `FormCell` behaviour). Second readings show small beneath.
  4. **What it counts:** thing chips, primary first. Tapping a chip shows its other
     counters ("rabbit: 羽, also 匹").
  5. Examples. 6. Watch out (`pitfalls`). 7. Related counters.
  8. Practise this counter (milestone 2).
- Speech reads the **kana** reading, never the kanji, so 一日 or 八本 can't come out wrong.
  `generate_audio.py` picks the readings up as texts.

**Counter quiz (milestone 2):** multiple choice, no timer, its own screen like Connections.

- *Which counter?* Show a thing (傘 + symbol), pick from four counters. Distractors come
  from other counters for similar-looking things.
- *Read it.* Show 三本, pick the reading. Distractors are the unchanged reading
  (さんほん), the neighbouring class's change (さんぽん) and a misplaced small っ
  (さっぽん). Any accepted reading counts as correct.
- A wrong answer names the pattern: "本 changes at 1, 3, 6, 8, 10: さんぼん".
- Attempts count toward streaks and totals. Counter Weak spots is a row on the quiz sheet
  when the Counters tab starts it.

**Milestone 3:**

- **Sound patterns:** one screen with a row per class, showing which numbers change.
  Tapping a column (6) highlights ろっ across the k and h classes.
- A **counter of the day** widget showing the readings grid. It opens via
  `verbtable://counter/<id>`.

### Content (milestone 1)

- The 18 counters above and 13 groups. Each counter has 3–6 things, 2–3 examples and at
  least one pitfall. Pitfalls include:
  - 4人 is よにん
  - 20歳 is はたち
  - 9時 is くじ
  - 3階 is さんがい
  - 一日 is ついたち as a date but いちにち as a length of time
  - when unsure, つ works up to 10
- Written by the assistant and unreviewed until the owner or a native speaker checks it.
  Every `data/` change is re-signed by the owner before merge.

## Milestones

1. **Reference and playground:** counters.json with groups, generation and validation,
   sync, models, search, routes, the Counters tab with the groups grid and group tables,
   the counter page with Count them.
2. **Counter quiz:** the `subject` rename, two question kinds, Weak spots for readings.
3. **Patterns and widget.**

## Testing

- **Python (`test_update_data.py`):** every class's generated table against the
  hand-written expected readings for one counter each (本, 分, 個, 冊, 枚); overrides
  replace generated keys; `changed` for each class; validation failures (missing key,
  unknown class, irregular without full overrides, katakana reading, bad `related`).
- **VerbKit:** decoding (including old manifests without `counters`); sync like grammar
  (manifest, signature, hash); `CounterSearch` on thing meaning, kana, romaji and
  counter; route URL round trip; milestone 2: the attempt migration keeps old rows,
  question building with all accepted readings correct, and distractors never equal to
  an accepted reading.
- **App:** simulator checks of the tab, search, counter page, playground animation and
  speech. iPad and macOS checks stay parked until the owner resumes them.

## As built (milestone 1)

What changed from the design while building it, and what has not been checked.

- **Pitfalls have their own type.** `CounterPitfall` replaces `GrammarPitfall` because a pitfall
  example on the 日 pages must be able to read 一日 as ついたち. Python's counted-run check already
  honoured those overrides; the Swift model would have dropped them.
- **Search noise rule is wider than the spec's.** A hiragana query that is a prefix of a base number
  or of a small-っ stem (さん, いっ, じゅっ) matches no forms, not only the base numbers themselves.
- **Navigation.** The sidebar sets the destination and switches the compact column; a group's
  counter headers, the full list and related counters push inside the detail stack. A new choice in
  the sidebar resets that stack. Search uses the same inline search and level scope bar as Grammar.
- **Group table.** At accessibility text sizes even a three-counter group scrolls sideways so text
  wraps instead of truncating. Counters that the level settings hide, and groups left empty by it, are
  left out.
- **Playground.** The 何 chip toggles between 何 and the last number. The readings grid rings the
  number the playground shows.
- **Audio.** `collect_texts` also takes `counters.json` and the furigana readings. Every reading, every
  thing's kana and every example are texts. An example with its own `readings` is spoken from its kana,
  and the app does the same, so the voice cannot pick the wrong reading.
- **Data.** 55 counted runs (二本, 三本, …) and the counter and example kanji were added to
  `furigana.json`. The content is the assistant's and unreviewed.

Not checked on a device or simulator: VoiceOver, Reduce Motion, iPad, macOS, the Spotlight results,
hiding N5 in Settings, a Forms search result landing on its cell, and the 人, 時, 円, 階 and days pages.
In the simulator the counters came from a temporary seed because `counters.json` is not published
yet; examples there read さんほん until the new `furigana.json` entries are published, but the
pipeline check guarantees the file itself reads さんぼん.

## Out of scope

- Numbers above 10 and compound readings (21本, 100本).
- Months and other durations (〜月, 〜ヶ月, 〜週間, 〜時間). Days and years are in.
- Dates above the 10th (はつか, じゅうよっか).
- A counter field on Word Bank entries.
- Recorded native audio and pitch accent.
- Typed answers in the quiz.
